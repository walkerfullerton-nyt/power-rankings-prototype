MLB Power Rankings: Every team has at least one reason to believe (right?)

At Power Rankings HQ, we're always wearing rose-colored glasses. This week, we're highlighting one reason to believe in each team. As the fictional Ted Lasso once eloquently declared, "Belief doesn't just happen because you hang something on a wall. It comes from your heart and head and gut."

All stats are through Monday unless otherwise noted.

1
— No change
Atlanta Braves
28-13 · Avg vote 1.0
One reason to believe "Just one?"

It's not hard to believe in the team that's No.1 in our Power Rankings, so I guess the question is: Is there reason to believe this is going to last? I'll answer with another question: What about this strong start is unsustainable? Bryce Elder is a surprise Cy Young candidate, but he was an All-Star in 2023. It's not like he's come out of nowhere. Matt Olson is off to a career-best pace, but he led the league in doubles last year and in homers in 2023.

— Chad Jennings

New York Yankees
26-16 · Avg vote 2.5
One reason to believe "The pieces are there, and they look good, too"

Carlos Rodón returned from the IL this weekend and averaged almost 96 mph on his fastball. Gerrit Cole just made his fifth minor-league rehab start and got up to 77 pitches. The Yankees could soon have both of them in a rotation that already has Max Fried and Cam Schlittler pitching as well as anyone in the American League. Offensively, Aaron Judge is as good as ever, Cody Bellinger is even better than he was last season, and Ben Rice leads the majors in wRC+.

— Chad Jennings

The Athletic
3
— No change
Los Angeles Dodgers
24-17 · Avg vote 2.8
One reason to believe "Plenty's going wrong for them, and they're still in first"

You can tell that something's going wrong with the Dodgers because they have competition. The Padres are matching them win for win, which isn't part of the plan. But Shohei Ohtani has been in an extended slump (.683 OPS since April 19), Kyle Tucker has been underwhelming and Tyler Glasnow is on the IL. Imagine what happens when Ohtani really starts hitting.

— Grant Brisbee
A
The Athletic

Milwaukee Brewers
22-16 · Avg vote 5.5
One reason to believe "The pitching staff is nails"

Jacob Misiorowski has become appointment viewing — he averaged 101.1 mph on 57 fastballs against the Yankees on Friday night. No one has touched his off-speed stuff either. The rest of the staff isn't far behind: Chad Patrick, Aaron Ashby, and Shane Drohan have built a 3.43 ERA, and the Brewers traded Burnes and Peralta — and didn't get relegated.

— Zack Meisel
A
The Athletic

Detroit Tigers
19-22 · Avg vote 9.5
One reason to believe "It won't get worse than this"

If the Tigers can merely survive for now, eventually their injured list won't be so crowded. Thankfully, the AL Central acts as a defibrillator, life vest and oxygen tank all rolled into one. If Detroit can weather Tarik Skubal's absence, Framber Valdez's antics, and injuries to Casey Mize, Verlander, Kerry Carpenter, and Gleyber Torres, perhaps the club can peak at the right time.

— Zack Meisel
A
The Athletic

▲ 6
Chicago White Sox
19-21 · Avg vote 20.3
One reason to believe "The White Sox have emerged from the darkness"

These aren't your slightly older sibling's White Sox. They survived the bleak summers, with 100 (or 121) losses and little hope. Some of the prospects they've been amassing have reached the majors and, in the case of Colson Montgomery, have thrived. Mix in a breakout season for Davis Martin and a jaw-dropping start for Munetaka Murakami, and maybe this is as simple as a young team playing free and easy.

— Zack Meisel
